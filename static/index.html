How the search works & scientific scope

The algorithm extends peptides on both sides of each supplied site and includes the site residue itself. It tests exactly one glycan per peptide, even when a peptide contains other potential sites. Isobaric sequences and site assignments may tie; ranking is not a probability.

Proton-only mode uses [M + zH]ᶻ⁺ or [M − zH]ᶻ⁻. The adduct library also supports solvent/metal adducts and multimers. Each selected adduct has its own neutral target: (observed m/z × charge − adduct mass shift) / multimer count. No isotope correction, protease digestion, disulfides, cysteine alkylation, other modifications, or multiple glycans per monomer are modeled. O-linked support is limited to S/T. No MS/MS localization or glycan structure identification is performed. UniProt annotations can be experimental or inferred; their evidence codes are retained. Missing annotations do not prove absence of glycosylation. Uncertain positions and unsupported residue types are listed separately. Different isoform sequences do not inherit canonical site positions.

The main Δm/z is the absolute difference between calculated and observed m/z. Signed Δm/z = calculated − observed; ppm = signed Δm/z ÷ observed m/z × 10⁶. ΔMass is the neutral mass difference in Da. Tolerance may be specified in ppm, m/z, or neutral-mass Da. The closest 25 candidates per measurement are shown, including clearly labeled out-of-tolerance candidates. All in-tolerance candidates are counted. CSV exports the displayed candidates, calculation totals, glycan selections, and search inputs. “View calculation” shows the amino acid counts and water corrections used for any candidate.
